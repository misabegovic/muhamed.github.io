# https://www.anthropic.com/news/position-open-weights-models

> Note: this snapshot is a fetched rendering (agent-summarized structure plus close-paraphrase quotes), not a verbatim copy of the page. Quotes below should be treated as near-verbatim.

**Published:** July 27, 2026 (three days after the Nvidia-hosted industry letter of July 24).

## Opening

"Anthropic has never advocated for a ban on open-weights models." Amodei states he opposes protectionist bans as ineffective policy.

## Core arguments

Two stated national-security concerns:

1. **Primary:** authoritarian governments developing superior AI for military dominance or internal repression.
2. **Secondary:** misuse of powerful models for cyberattacks or biological weapons, where open-weights models may present higher risk because usage cannot be monitored.

## Three policy proposals

1. **Chip restrictions** — prevent sales of advanced semiconductor technology to China and crack down on smuggling; China lacks domestic capacity for frontier training.
2. **Distillation crackdowns** — "industrial-scale distillation" operations that extract capabilities from existing models should face policy intervention, as they let authoritarian states partially circumvent chip limits.
3. **Mandatory safety testing** — all sufficiently capable models, "open and closed," should undergo mandatory testing for cyber, biological, and alignment risks before release. Acknowledges: "to be effective, testing would need to be global, which means even the CCP would need to be on board."

## On the industry letter

Acknowledges open-weights benefits (access, competition, user control) but disputes that open weights necessarily improve defense or safety: "seems at least as likely to me that the opposite will be true." Argues questions "should be empirically answered by rigorous pre-release testing, not assumed in advance." Raises a biological offense/defense asymmetry: advanced models "may be able to quickly weaponize" pathogens faster than defenses respond.

On competition: "It _would_ protect US AI companies from competition, but that has never been my goal."

## Closing

"We should instead focus on keeping powerful chips out of authoritarian hands, stopping industrial-scale distillation, and requiring safety testing of all sufficiently capable models, open and closed."

## Notable absences

Names no competitors (no DeepSeek, no Meta/Llama, no Kimi); discusses "Chinese open-weights models" abstractly. Does not engage the letter's concentration-risk argument (a few closed labs as single points of failure).
